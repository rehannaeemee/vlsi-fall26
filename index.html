Course overview
Intel 4004 Microprocessor

Designing the systems behind modern computing.

Very Large Scale Integration is the process of combining millions—or billions—of transistors on a single chip. It enables powerful processors, memories, and systems-on-chip while balancing speed, energy, area, and cost.

This course introduces VLSI system design from technology scaling through architecture optimization. Students investigate device-, circuit-, and system-level trade-offs through focused analysis and HDL-based design tasks.
In the CLASS, students will explore how modern chips are built, starting from the behavior of a single transistor and moving toward complete digital systems. They will learn CMOS logic, digital circuit design, timing, power, and area concepts. The course also shows how these ideas come together in the design of processors, accelerators, and other high-performance integrated circuits.
In the LAB, students will put these concepts into practice using modern VLSI design and simulation tools. They will design, simulate, synthesize, and implement digital circuits while seeing how real engineering decisions affect speed, power, and chip area. The lab will give them a practical introduction to the design flow used in the semiconductor industry and prepare them for further study, research, and careers in chip design.

What you will learn
01

Understand scaling

Connect semiconductor technology trends with circuit behavior and system-level consequences.

02

Design in HDL

Build synthesizable hardware descriptions and reason about the resulting implementation.

03

Optimize PPA

Evaluate and improve performance, power, and area across competing microarchitectures.

04

Use CAD flows

Move from RTL through synthesis and implementation while reading reports critically.
